Google reviews are one of the highest-leverage things a trade business can invest in — and most businesses treat them as an afterthought.

Here's why they matter more than almost anything else in your marketing:

  • A business with 50 reviews at 4.8 stars beats a competitor with 5 reviews in the local pack almost every time — regardless of who spends more on ads
  • 83% of consumers say online reviews influence their purchase decisions (BrightLocal, 2024)
  • Google's local ranking algorithm explicitly weights review quantity, recency, and rating in local search placement
  • New reviews (within the last 90 days) carry significantly more weight than old ones

The gap between trade businesses with great review profiles and those without isn't talent — it's process. Here's a repeatable system you can put in place today.

Step 1: Fix the timing — ask right after the job

The single biggest mistake: asking for a review at the wrong moment.

Asking verbally at the end of a job ("feel free to leave us a review!") produces maybe a 5–10% conversion rate. Customers have good intentions and no follow-through. By the time they get home, make dinner, put the kids to bed, and remember they wanted to leave a review, days have passed and the impulse is gone.

The right window is 2–4 hours after job completion — when the customer is back to their normal life, the work is fresh in their mind, and they're satisfied but not yet distracted by something else. A text message in this window consistently outperforms every other ask method.

Step 2: Make it dead simple with a direct link

Every extra step you add drops your conversion rate. "Google us and find the review tab" might sound straightforward, but for someone who's not technically savvy it's 4–5 steps. Most won't complete it.

Instead, send a direct link to your Google review form. To get this link:

  1. Search your business name in Google
  2. Find your Google Business Profile on the right side of the results
  3. Click "Get more reviews" — Google gives you a short link you can copy
  4. That link drops the customer directly into the review box, one tap away

Shorten it with a URL shortener if you want something cleaner for text messages.

Step 3: Write a message that doesn't feel like a form letter

The review request message matters. A generic "Please leave us a review" feels transactional. A personal message feels like a real business that cares.

A template that works well for trade businesses:

Hi [First Name], thanks for having us out today. We hope the [service] is working great for you! If you have 60 seconds, it would mean a lot to us if you could leave a quick Google review — it helps other homeowners in [city] find a trustworthy [trade]. Here's the link: [link] — Thanks, [Your Name] at [Company]

Key elements: personalized with first name, references the specific service, explains why reviews help (not just "helps us"), and uses the direct link.

Step 4: Follow up once (but only once)

Some customers genuinely meant to leave a review and forgot. A single follow-up 48–72 hours later — not guilt-tripping, just a gentle reminder — can recover another 10–15% of reviews from customers who didn't act on the first message.

Template:

Hi [First Name], just checking in to make sure everything with your [service] is still going well. If you do have a moment to leave a quick review, we'd really appreciate it: [link]. And if anything wasn't right, please reply to this text and we'll make it right. — [Company]

Note: this follow-up does double duty. It shows the customer you care about their experience after the job, not just during it. That builds loyalty regardless of whether they leave a review.

Step 5: Respond to every review — positive and negative

Responding to reviews signals to Google that you're an active, engaged business — and it signals to prospective customers that you care.

For 5-star reviews, a short personalized response is better than a copy-paste template:

"Thank you so much, [Name]! It was a pleasure working with you on the [service]. Give us a call anytime — we're always here."

For negative reviews (which will eventually happen to every business), respond calmly and professionally:

"Hi [Name], I'm sorry to hear your experience didn't meet our standards. This doesn't reflect how we typically work. Please call us at [number] — we'd like to make this right."

A professional, empathetic response to a negative review often helps more than it hurts — it shows potential customers that you handle problems like a legitimate business, not by getting defensive or disappearing.

Step 6: Automate the whole thing

The system above works. The problem: most trade business owners are in the field, not managing follow-up texts. A job ends, you move to the next one, the review window closes, and you collect nothing.

The businesses that consistently accumulate reviews don't remember to send texts — they've set up automation that does it for them. Trigger the message when a job is marked complete in your field service software, your CRM, or even when a payment is processed. No manual steps.

Even without software integration, a simple scheduled text sequence can be set up in tools like Google Messages, Podium, or FieldSprout's automated review request flow — which sends the request, handles the follow-up, and logs the response without you touching a thing.

How many reviews do you need to see the difference?

You don't need hundreds. In most mid-size markets, moving from 5 reviews to 25–30 recent reviews at 4.5+ stars is enough to see measurable improvement in your local pack ranking and click-through rate. In competitive metros, you may need 50–100 to consistently appear in the top three.

The math: at a 20–25% automation conversion rate, a business completing 4 jobs per day can generate 20–30 new reviews per month on autopilot. That's a dominant review profile within 60–90 days for almost any local market.

Start the system today. The competitors who are ranking above you probably started this 6 months ago — but you can close the gap faster than it took them to build it.
