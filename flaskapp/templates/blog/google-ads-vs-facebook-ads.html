Every trade contractor eventually hits the same question: should I be running Google Ads, Facebook Ads, or both?

The honest answer depends on what you're selling, how urgent the need is, and where you are in your marketing maturity. Here's the breakdown that most agencies won't give you — because they make money either way.

The Core Difference: Capturing vs. Creating Demand

Google Ads captures existing demand. Someone searches "emergency AC repair Dallas" — they already want to buy. Your ad shows up, they click, they call. The intent is baked in.

Facebook Ads create demand. You show an ad to a homeowner who isn't currently thinking about HVAC, but whose system is 12 years old. You plant a seed. Maybe they call this month, maybe they call in six months when something goes wrong.

Neither is better in the abstract. They solve different problems.

When Google Ads Wins

Google is the clear choice when:

  • Urgency drives decisions. Plumbing emergencies, HVAC failures, electrical hazards — these are search-first situations. Nobody scrolls Instagram when their basement is flooding.
  • Your ticket size is high. High-value jobs ($2,000+) justify higher CPCs because the ROI per conversion is large. Google CPCs for competitive trades can run $15–$40, which only makes sense if the job is worth hundreds or thousands.
  • You want predictable lead volume. Google Search is more consistent because it's tied to search volume, which is relatively stable month-to-month in your market.
  • You're just starting out. For a new campaign with a limited budget, Google gives you faster feedback — you know quickly if you're hitting target CPL or not.

When Facebook Ads Win

Facebook (Meta) makes more sense when:

  • You're selling planned services. Lawn care, window replacement, solar, exterior painting — these aren't emergency decisions. Facebook's awareness model works better when buyers have time to consider.
  • You have creative content. Before/after photos, job site videos, and customer testimonial clips perform extremely well on Facebook and Instagram. If you have this material, Meta's visual format converts it into leads efficiently.
  • You want to dominate your local market. Facebook's geographic and demographic targeting is excellent. For a contractor who only works in a 15-mile radius, running Facebook ads to homeowners 30–65 in that zip code is very efficient.
  • You're retargeting website visitors. Facebook's pixel retargeting is one of the best ROI moves in digital marketing. A homeowner who visited your pricing page and didn't call can be followed for 30–90 days at a very low cost.

Real Numbers: CPL and Conversion Rates

These are typical ranges for trade contractors. Markets vary significantly:

Metric Google Ads Facebook Ads
Average CPL $50–$120 $25–$65
Lead-to-booked-job rate 35–55% 15–30%
Cost per booked job $100–$300 $100–$350
Best for High-urgency, high-ticket Planned work, retargeting
Time to optimize 6–12 weeks 3–6 weeks

Notice that cost per booked job is similar once you account for conversion rate differences. Facebook leads are cheaper but closer — they need more nurturing before they schedule. Google leads are more expensive but more ready.

The Right Order: Start With Google

If you can only run one, start with Google Ads. Here's why:

  1. You're capturing buyers who are already in market — the lowest-hanging fruit
  2. The feedback loop is faster — you know within 30–60 days if it's working
  3. It establishes your baseline CPL, which makes evaluating Facebook ROI much easier

Once Google is profitable and you have a baseline CPL, add Facebook with a specific goal: retargeting your Google visitors, or running awareness campaigns in your core zip codes. At this point, the two platforms compound each other — someone who saw your Facebook ad is more likely to click your Google ad when they eventually search.

The Combination Play

The most effective setup for a mature trade marketing operation:

  • Google Search — capture emergency and high-intent searches
  • Google Local Service Ads — appear above Search results with the Google Guaranteed badge
  • Facebook/Instagram retargeting — follow up with website visitors for 30–60 days
  • Facebook awareness — seasonal campaigns to homeowners in your service area
  • Google Demand Gen — intercept competitor switchers on YouTube and Gmail

You don't need all of these on day one. But knowing the full picture helps you build in the right order instead of jumping between platforms wondering why nothing sticks.

The Bottom Line

Google Ads wins for emergency trades and high-urgency services. Facebook wins for planned work, retargeting, and brand awareness. For most trade contractors, Google comes first — then Facebook is added once you have the budget and the creative assets to use it well.

The worst move is spreading a small budget across both platforms before either has enough data to optimize. Pick one, do it right, then expand.
