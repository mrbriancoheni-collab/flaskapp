The most common reason trade contractors quit Google Ads before it works: they expected leads in week one, saw nothing, and pulled the budget.

Google Ads does work for trade businesses — but it operates on a timeline that has nothing to do with impatience. Here's what actually happens in the first 12 weeks, and how to know whether your campaign is on track.

Week 1–2: The Algorithm Is Blind

When you launch a new campaign, Google has zero data about which users, times of day, or search queries produce conversions for your business specifically. The algorithm starts in exploration mode — it's casting a wide net to gather information.

What to expect:

  • Some impressions, probably some clicks
  • Few or no leads
  • Higher cost-per-click than the account will eventually average

This is normal. Do not pause the campaign. Do not make major changes to bids or targeting. Let Google collect data.

What to check instead: Is your conversion tracking actually firing? Open Google Tag Assistant and fill out your own contact form. If a conversion doesn't register in the account within 24 hours, you have a tracking problem that will cripple the entire campaign — fix it before anything else.

Week 3–4: First Real Signal

By now you should be seeing impressions and clicks at a reasonable pace. Some leads will start trickling in. The cost-per-lead will be high — often 2–3x what it will be at 90 days.

Google's Smart Bidding algorithm needs approximately 30–50 conversions before it becomes statistically reliable. If your campaign is getting 5–10 leads per month, you're still in the early learning window. That's okay — just don't compare week-3 CPL to your long-term target.

What to check at week 4:

  • Review your Search Terms report — add negative keywords for irrelevant searches
  • Check impression share — if it's below 40%, your budget may be too low
  • Look at your top 3 ads' click-through rates — if CTR is below 3%, rewrite your weakest headline

Week 5–8: The Algorithm Starts Learning

This is the inflection point most contractors never reach because they quit too early.

With 15–30 conversions in the account, Google's bidding starts to tighten. It knows which users convert for you. CPL drops, click quality improves, and the percentage of your budget going to waste shrinks. You should see lead volume increase without a proportional spend increase.

If your CPL is still very high at week 8, the problem is usually one of three things:

  1. Budget too low — not enough clicks to generate conversion data
  2. Landing page weak — good clicks, poor conversion rate
  3. Wrong match types — broad keywords attracting non-service searches

Week 9–12: Optimization Window

By 90 days, a well-run campaign should be approaching target CPL. This is when strategic optimizations make the biggest difference:

  • Pause keywords with high spend and zero conversions
  • Increase bids on your top-converting ad groups
  • Add ad schedule adjustments (bid higher during your core service hours)
  • Test new ad copy against your current winners

The campaigns that reach this stage efficiently have one thing in common: they weren't touched constantly in the first 8 weeks. Every major change restarts the learning period. Stability in the early weeks is a feature, not impatience.

How to Tell If Your Campaign Is Broken vs. Just Slow

Symptom Slow (normal) Broken (fix now)
No conversions in week 1 ✓ Expected Tracking not set up
High CPL in weeks 2–4 ✓ Learning phase —
Zero impressions — Budget too low, ads disapproved, or wrong targeting
Lots of clicks, zero leads — Landing page or tracking problem
Impression share under 20% — Budget is too low for this market
CPL still high at week 10 — Structural problem — review keywords, ads, landing page

The Minimum Budget to See Results

In most trade markets, the minimum effective budget is $1,500–$2,500/month. Below this, you don't generate enough clicks for the algorithm to learn, and competitors with larger budgets win most auctions before yours even show.

The formula: target leads per month × local CPC × average clicks per lead. If your market's average CPC is $12 and it takes 4 clicks to generate one lead, getting 20 leads/month requires $960 in clicks — but with overhead (impressions that don't click, learning-phase waste), you need $1,500–$2,000 to actually hit 20 leads.

The Bottom Line

Google Ads for trade businesses is a 90-day investment, not a 7-day one. The contractors who win with it consistently are the ones who launched with the right setup, stayed patient through the learning phase, and made small, methodical optimizations starting around week 8.

If you're in week 2 wondering why you're not flooded with calls — you're not behind. You're right on schedule.
