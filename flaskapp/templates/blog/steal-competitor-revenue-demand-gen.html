Every month, a percentage of your competitors' customers get frustrated enough to start looking for alternatives. They search for things like "[Competitor] complaints" or "alternatives to [Competitor]" — and right now, nobody is showing them an ad for your business.

That gap is exactly what Google Demand Gen was built to close.

Why Competitor Switchers Are Your Best Leads

A customer in the middle of switching is almost always:

  • Already convinced they need the service
  • Frustrated enough with their current provider to act
  • Actively comparing options — ready to make a decision quickly

Compare that to cold traffic, which requires you to first convince someone they have a problem. Switchers already know. You just have to show up at the right moment and prove you're better.

Step 1: Catch the Switchers

Build a custom audience segment in Google Ads using "people who searched for any of these terms":

  • [Competitor name] pricing
  • [Competitor name] complaints
  • [Competitor name] reviews
  • [Competitor name] alternatives
  • cancel [Competitor name]
  • switch from [Competitor name]

You can include multiple competitors in a single segment. The more specific the signal, the higher the intent.

Step 2: Choose Your Channels

Demand Gen runs on YouTube, Gmail, and Google Discover — not the Search results page. This matters because you're not interrupting someone mid-task; you're reaching them during browsing and video consumption, when they're more receptive to brand messaging.

For trade businesses, YouTube pre-roll and Gmail ads tend to perform best. Gmail placement puts your offer directly in the inbox of people who have recently searched for competitor alternatives.

Step 3: One Ad Group Per Pain Point

Don't run one generic "we're better" campaign. Build a separate ad group for each specific frustration:

Ad Group Angle Landing Page Promise
Pricing Group "Tired of paying more every year?" Transparent pricing page with comparisons
Support Group "What if someone actually picked up the phone?" Testimonials about responsiveness
Lock-in Group "No contracts. Cancel anytime." Terms page + flexibility guarantee

Step 4: Match the Landing Page to the Ad

This is where most competitor campaigns fail. They run a compelling ad and then send traffic to a homepage that doesn't address the frustration at all.

Each ad group needs a dedicated landing page that:

  1. Opens with the exact pain the ad mentioned
  2. Presents social proof from customers who switched (and why)
  3. Shows a clear, specific differentiator with numbers
  4. Has one call-to-action above the fold

If someone searched for "[Competitor] contract complaints" and clicked your ad about "no contracts," the first thing they should see on your page is a headline like: "Month-to-month. No cancellation fees. Ever."

Step 5: Build a Lookalike from Your Converters

Once you have 100+ conversions from Demand Gen, upload your customer list to Google Ads as a "similar audiences" seed. Google will find new people who behave like your converters — dramatically expanding your reach beyond just people who searched for your specific competitors.

This turns your competitor targeting campaign into a self-improving lead engine: the more customers you win, the better Google gets at finding the next ones.

Budget and Expectations

Start with 10–15% of your total Google Ads budget. Demand Gen CPMs are lower than Search CPCs, so even $500–$800/month gives you meaningful reach in most trade markets.

Expect a 3–4 week ramp before the algorithm optimizes. Don't judge performance in week one. Measure at the 30 and 60-day marks, and optimize toward cost per lead — not impressions or clicks.

The Bottom Line

Your competitors are spending money to acquire customers. Some of those customers will become unhappy. Demand Gen lets you intercept them at the exact moment they start looking for a way out.

Most trade contractors have never run a Demand Gen campaign. That means the audience is underpriced and your ads face minimal competition. Right now is the best time to start — before your competitors figure this out too.
