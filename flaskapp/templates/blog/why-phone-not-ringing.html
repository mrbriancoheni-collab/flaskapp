You're good at what you do. Your past customers would recommend you in a heartbeat. But the phone just isn't ringing like it used to — or like it should.

Before you assume it's the economy or the season, consider this: most trade business owners who lose inbound calls lose them because of marketing gaps, not because demand dried up. Competitors with the same services in the same market are booking solid. The difference is almost always how they show up — online, in search, and in follow-up.

Here are the five gaps we see most often, and what to do about each.

Gap 1: Your Google Business Profile is incomplete or stale

When someone in your area searches "plumber near me" or "HVAC company in [city]," Google's local pack — the three businesses that appear above organic results — is where most of the calls go. If you're not in that box, you're invisible to the highest-intent buyers in your market.

What gets you there:

  • A fully completed profile — every field filled in, including services, service areas, hours, and photos
  • Recent reviews — Google heavily weights recency; a business with 10 reviews in the last 90 days outranks one with 200 reviews from 3 years ago
  • Regular posts and updates — treat your Google Business Profile like a social feed; even monthly updates signal to Google that you're active
  • Your primary keyword in your business description — "emergency plumber serving downtown Denver and surrounding areas" works better than a vague tagline

If your profile is set up and untouched, you're leaving calls on the table every single day.

Gap 2: You're spending on ads without the right targeting

Google Ads can be the fastest way to get the phone ringing — or the fastest way to burn through $500 with nothing to show for it. The difference is in the details.

The most common mistakes trade businesses make with Google Ads:

  • Broad match keywords that trigger irrelevant searches ("plumber" matching "plumber apprentice jobs")
  • No negative keyword list — paying for every search that mentions your trade, including DIY tutorials and competitor brand terms
  • Landing on the homepage instead of a dedicated service page
  • No call tracking, so you can't tell which ads are actually driving calls
  • Set-it-and-forget-it campaigns that never get optimized

If you connect Google Ads and don't continuously manage it, performance degrades within 30–60 days as auction dynamics shift. The businesses winning in local paid search are optimizing constantly — adjusting bids, cutting waste, testing new ad copy.

Gap 3: You have no review generation system

When two contractors show up in the same search, the one with more recent 5-star reviews almost always gets the call — even if they charge more. Reviews are trust at scale. A new customer who doesn't know you will look at your star rating before they ever dial your number.

The problem isn't that customers don't want to leave reviews. Most happy customers would be glad to help if you asked at the right moment, the right way.

The gap: most trade businesses rely on asking verbally at the end of a job, which maybe 5–10% of customers actually follow through on. A simple automated text sent 2–4 hours after job completion — while the experience is still fresh — can increase your review capture rate to 25–40%.

Businesses that consistently generate new reviews also rank higher in Google Maps organically, which compounds the benefit over time.

Gap 4: New leads aren't getting followed up fast enough

According to data from Harvard Business Review, businesses that respond to leads within 5 minutes are 100x more likely to connect than those who wait an hour. In the trades, where customers often have an urgent problem and are calling multiple contractors, speed to respond is everything.

But most trade businesses are in the field when leads come in. A web form fills out at 2pm while you're on a job, and by the time you see it at 6pm, that customer has already booked someone else.

The fix is an automated first response — a text that goes out within 60 seconds acknowledging the inquiry, telling them you'll follow up shortly, and ideally letting them book a call or estimate on the spot. Even a simple "Hi, this is [Company]. We got your request and will call you within the hour — is there a good number to reach you?" doubles contact rates.

Gap 5: Your website doesn't rank for the services you actually want to sell

Most trade business websites have a homepage and maybe a few generic service pages. But the searches that drive the highest-intent calls are hyper-specific: "water heater replacement cost Dallas," "emergency AC repair Phoenix," "licensed electrician panel upgrade Chicago."

If your website doesn't have pages targeting those specific queries in your specific city, you won't rank for them — and you'll rely entirely on paid ads for every call. Organic search traffic is free, compounds over time, and generates some of the highest-intent leads you can get.

Building these pages doesn't require a blog or a marketing degree. It requires a structured page for each core service + location combination, with the right copy, technical signals, and internal links.

The fix: cover all five, consistently

None of these five gaps are complicated to understand. The challenge is that fixing them — and keeping them working — takes consistent attention across multiple channels simultaneously.
