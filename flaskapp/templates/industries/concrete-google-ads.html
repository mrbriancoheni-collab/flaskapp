Why Concrete Google Ads Are Different

Concrete contractors face unique Google Ads challenges that generic marketing agencies don't understand. Your business is weather-dependent—you can't pour concrete in freezing temperatures, making winter slow in cold climates. Your customer intent varies dramatically between someone researching "concrete driveway cost" (early planning phase) versus "concrete contractor near me" (ready to book).

Different concrete services have vastly different margins and project values. A $500 crack repair versus a $15,000 stamped concrete patio require completely different bidding strategies, ad messaging, and landing pages. Generic Google Ads strategies fail because they treat all concrete searches the same.

The Concrete Google Ads Problem: Wrong Traffic Mix

Most concrete companies waste 35-55% of their Google Ads budget on irrelevant searches—DIYers looking for bags of concrete mix, homeowners pricing out ready-mix delivery for their own pours, commercial/industrial contractors (when you're residential-focused), and people just gathering rough pricing with no intention to hire yet.

The most common concrete Google Ads mistakes:

  • Missing DIY negatives — wasting spend on "concrete bags", "how to pour concrete", "DIY driveway"
  • Attracting materials buyers — broad match bringing in "ready mix concrete price", "concrete delivery"
  • No project-type segmentation — driveways, patios, stamped concrete all in one campaign
  • Ignoring weather patterns — running full budgets in winter when work stops (cold climates)
  • Generic ad copy — "concrete services" instead of specific project messaging
  • No visual proof — missing landing page galleries of completed driveways and patios

1. Seasonal & Weather-Based Budget Optimization

The Strategic Director agent monitors weather forecasts, seasonal patterns, and your booking capacity to automatically shift budgets throughout the year.

Cold climate seasonal budget allocation:

  • Spring (March-May): Peak budget—homeowners planning summer projects, ideal weather conditions
  • Summer (June-August): Maintain high budget—active construction season, project execution
  • Fall (September-October): Moderate budget—rush to complete before first freeze
  • Winter (November-February): Minimal budget (20-30% of peak)—planning only, no active pours

Warm climate (Arizona, Texas, Florida, Southern California): More consistent year-round with summer budget reductions (too hot for comfortable work) and spring/fall peaks.

2. DIY and Materials-Only Filtering

One of the biggest concrete Google Ads wastes is attracting DIYers and people buying materials, not hiring contractors.

The Negative Keyword Agent automatically blocks:

  • DIY terms: "DIY", "how to", "yourself", "tutorial", "YouTube"
  • Materials only: "bags", "price per yard", "ready mix", "concrete delivery", "bulk concrete"
  • Tools/equipment: "concrete mixer", "forms", "tools", "rental", "equipment"
  • Unwanted project types: "commercial", "industrial", "sidewalk" (if not offering)
  • Job seekers: "concrete jobs", "hiring", "careers", "salary"

These negatives typically save $400-700 per month for concrete contractors running $3,000-6,000/month budgets.

Common Concrete Google Ads Questions

Should concrete companies run Google Ads in winter?

In cold climates: Reduce budgets by 70-80% but don't pause entirely. Focus on planning/design consultations for spring projects and indoor/garage floor work (less weather-dependent).

In warm climates: Run year-round with minor seasonal adjustments. Some areas actually prefer winter concrete work (cooler temperatures, better curing conditions).

How much should concrete companies spend on Google Ads?

A typical starting budget is $2,500-5,000/month during peak season. With average project values of $4,000-10,000 and 40-50% margins, you can afford $60-120 per lead and remain profitable.

Target CPL by project type:

  • Repairs: $20-40 per lead
  • Standard driveways/patios: $40-80 per lead
  • Stamped/decorative: $80-150 per lead

What's a good conversion rate for concrete Google Ads?

Repair campaigns: 8-15% conversion rate (form fill or call)
Driveway/patio campaigns: 5-10% conversion rate
Stamped/decorative campaigns: 4-8% conversion rate (longer decision timeline)

If you're below these benchmarks, likely issues:

  • Irrelevant traffic (missing negative keywords for DIY, materials)
  • Weak landing pages (no portfolio images, unclear pricing)
  • Generic messaging (not speaking to specific project types)

Should concrete companies use Google Local Services Ads?

Yes, especially for repairs and smaller projects. Google Local Services Ads work well for concrete contractors because they appear above regular ads and charge per lead (not per click). Best for repair services and quick-turnaround projects. Less effective for large custom projects where design consultation is needed.
