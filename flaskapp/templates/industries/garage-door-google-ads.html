Why Garage Door Google Ads Are Different

Garage door companies face unique Google Ads challenges that generic marketing agencies don't understand. Your customers search differently depending on whether they have an emergency (door won't close, broken spring), need routine service (opener replacement, maintenance), or want a new door installation. Your margins vary dramatically between a $200 spring replacement and a $2,500 new door installation.

Generic Google Ads strategies fail because they don't account for these garage door-specific realities. You need campaigns that separate true emergencies from planned installations. You need aggressive negative keyword strategies to block DIY searchers looking for YouTube tutorials on how to replace springs themselves. And you need smart geographic targeting to avoid unprofitable long-distance service calls.

The Garage Door Google Ads Problem: DIY Traffic Waste

Most garage door companies overspend on Google Ads by attracting DIY searchers, people shopping for replacement springs only, and job seekers instead of customers ready to hire a professional. They run generic "garage door services" campaigns that waste 40-60% of ad spend on clicks that will never convert.

The most common garage door Google Ads mistakes:

  • Mixing emergency and installation in the same campaigns with same bids
  • Broad match keywords like "garage door" that attract DIY searchers and parts shoppers
  • No negative keywords for "DIY", "YouTube", "parts", "springs only", "how to"
  • Generic ad copy that doesn't emphasize same-day service or safety concerns
  • No service area targeting leading to unprofitable distant service calls
  • Not separating residential from commercial (different audiences and margins)

Common Garage Door Google Ads Questions

Should garage door companies advertise residential and commercial separately?

Yes, if you serve both. Residential and commercial garage door work are different businesses with different keywords, decision-makers, and project sizes. If you're residential-only (most small garage door companies), add "commercial" as a negative keyword.

How much should garage door companies spend on Google Ads?

A typical rule of thumb: $35-70 per lead target for emergency repairs, $60-120 per lead for new door installations. If your average spring replacement generates $250 in revenue at 55% margin, you can afford $50-70 per lead and still be profitable.

Most garage door companies spend $2,000-5,000 per month on Google Ads, with larger companies in competitive markets spending $5,000-10,000/month.

What's a good conversion rate for garage door Google Ads?

Emergency repair campaigns: 10-18% conversion rate (form fill or call)
Opener campaigns: 8-14% conversion rate
New door installation campaigns: 5-10% conversion rate

If you're below these benchmarks, you likely have issues with:

  • Irrelevant traffic (poor keyword targeting or missing negative keywords)
  • Weak landing pages (slow load time, poor mobile experience, unclear CTA)
  • Misaligned ad copy (promise doesn't match landing page)

Should garage door companies use Google Local Services Ads (LSAs)?

Absolutely. Google Local Services Ads (the "Google Guaranteed" listings) are extremely effective for garage door companies. They appear above regular Google Ads and you only pay per lead, not per click. LSAs work especially well for emergency repairs and general garage door service.

FieldSprout manages both Google Ads and Google Local Services Ads in a coordinated strategy. LSAs handle branded and emergency searches, while Google Ads expand reach for specific services like new door installation and opener replacement.
