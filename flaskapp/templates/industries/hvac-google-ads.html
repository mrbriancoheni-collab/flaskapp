Why HVAC Google Ads Are Different

HVAC contractors face unique Google Ads challenges that generic marketing agencies don't understand. Your business is seasonal—AC service peaks in summer, furnace work peaks in winter. Your customers search differently depending on whether they need emergency repairs or preventive maintenance. And your margins vary dramatically between a $150 repair call and a $12,000 system replacement.

Generic Google Ads strategies fail because they don't account for these HVAC-specific realities. You need intelligent budget allocation that shifts spend from heating to cooling (and back) based on the season. You need separate campaigns for emergency vs. scheduled work. And you need aggressive negative keyword strategies to block the tire-kickers who have no intention of calling a professional.

The HVAC Google Ads Problem: Seasonal Waste

Most HVAC companies overspend on Google Ads during shoulder seasons (spring and fall) when demand is low, then get outbid during peak seasons when demand is high. They run generic "HVAC services" campaigns that attract DIY searchers, competitors doing research, and people 3-6 months away from making a decision.

The most common HVAC Google Ads mistakes:

  • Running flat budgets year-round instead of seasonal adjustments
  • Mixing emergency and maintenance in the same campaigns
  • Broad match keywords like "air conditioning" that attract DIYers
  • No negative keywords for "DIY", "YouTube", "parts", "jobs"
  • Generic ad copy that doesn't speak to emergency vs. planned work
  • No ZIP-level targeting to protect margins on long-distance calls

  • Maintenance searches need different ad copy focused on value, not urgency
  • Conversion tracking is different (immediate booking vs. scheduled appointment)

FieldSprout automatically structures your campaigns into:

  • AC Maintenance & Tune-Ups — "AC tune-up", "AC service", "AC maintenance" (moderate bids, value messaging)

3. HVAC-Specific Negative Keyword Strategy

The Negative Keyword Agent knows HVAC search patterns. It automatically blocks:

  • DIY searches: "DIY", "how to fix", "YouTube", "tutorial", "myself"

Common HVAC Google Ads Questions

Should I run Google Ads in the off-season?

Yes, but with lower budgets. Even in off-season months, there are emergency repairs and people planning ahead for system replacements. The key is adjusting budgets down (not pausing entirely) and focusing on high-intent keywords only.

FieldSprout automatically reduces budgets by 40-60% during shoulder seasons while maintaining presence for emergency and installation searches.

How much should HVAC companies spend on Google Ads?

A typical rule of thumb: $30-50 per lead target for emergency/repair work, $100-200 per lead for installations. If your average emergency repair generates $400 in revenue at 50% margin, you can afford $50-80 per lead and still be profitable.

Most HVAC companies spend $2,500-7,000 per month on Google Ads during peak season, scaling down to $1,000-3,000 during off-season.

What's a good conversion rate for HVAC Google Ads?

Emergency repair campaigns: 8-15% conversion rate (form fill or call)
Maintenance campaigns: 5-10% conversion rate
Installation campaigns: 3-7% conversion rate

If you're below these benchmarks, you likely have issues with:

  • Irrelevant traffic (poor keyword targeting or missing negative keywords)
  • Weak landing pages (slow load time, poor mobile experience, unclear CTA)
  • Misaligned ad copy (promise doesn't match landing page)

Should HVAC companies use Google Local Services Ads (LSAs)?

Yes. Google Local Services Ads (the "Google Guaranteed" listings at the very top) are extremely effective for HVAC contractors. They appear above regular Google Ads and you only pay per lead, not per click.

FieldSprout manages both Google Ads and Google Local Services Ads in a coordinated strategy. LSAs handle branded and high-intent searches, while Google Ads expand reach for competitive keywords and seasonal services.
