Why Irrigation Google Ads Are Different

Irrigation contractors face unique Google Ads challenges that generic marketing agencies don't understand. Your business is highly seasonal—new system installations peak in spring when homeowners are landscaping, repairs spike in summer when systems break down, and winterization services cluster in fall before the first freeze. Your customer intent varies dramatically between a homeowner with broken sprinkler heads versus someone planning a $5,000 complete system installation.

Generic Google Ads strategies fail because they don't account for these irrigation-specific realities. You need intelligent budget allocation that shifts spend from installation campaigns (spring) to repair campaigns (summer) to winterization (fall). And you need aggressive negative keyword strategies to block agricultural irrigation searchers, drip irrigation kit shoppers, and DIYers who have no intention of hiring a professional.

The Irrigation Google Ads Problem: Wrong Traffic

Most irrigation companies waste 30-50% of their Google Ads budget on irrelevant searches—agricultural/farm irrigation, DIY drip irrigation kits, commercial/industrial systems (when they serve residential), and parts-only shoppers. They run flat budgets year-round despite massive seasonal demand swings, and use generic "irrigation services" ad copy that doesn't speak to specific customer needs.

The most common irrigation Google Ads mistakes:

  • Missing agricultural negatives — wasting spend on farm/commercial irrigation searchers
  • Attracting DIY kit buyers — broad match keywords bringing in "drip irrigation kit" searches
  • Flat year-round budgets — not scaling for seasonal demand peaks
  • Mixing repairs and installations — different intent levels in the same campaigns
  • No winterization campaigns — missing profitable fall seasonal service revenue
  • Generic ad copy — "irrigation services" instead of specific problem-solving messaging

1. Seasonal Budget Optimization

The Strategic Director agent monitors seasonal patterns and your booking capacity to automatically shift budgets between installation, repair, and winterization campaigns throughout the year.

Typical seasonal budget allocation:

  • Spring (March-May): 40% installations, 30% repairs, 20% spring activation, 10% maintenance
  • Summer (June-August): 20% installations, 50% repairs, 20% maintenance, 10% upgrades
  • Fall (September-November): 10% installations, 25% repairs, 50% winterization, 15% maintenance
  • Winter (December-February): 30% planning/installations, 20% repairs (warm climates), 30% maintenance contracts, 20% equipment upgrades

This isn't a simple calendar-based rule. The AI considers weather patterns, your current booking capacity, and local market conditions.

2. Residential vs. Agricultural Filtering

One of the biggest irrigation Google Ads wastes is attracting agricultural, farm, or commercial irrigation searches when you only serve residential customers (or vice versa).

The Negative Keyword Agent automatically blocks:

  • Agricultural terms: "farm", "agricultural", "field", "crop", "pasture", "ranch"
  • Commercial/industrial: "commercial irrigation", "golf course", "park irrigation" (unless you serve these)
  • DIY products: "drip irrigation kit", "soaker hose", "irrigation timer", "DIY sprinkler"
  • Parts only: "sprinkler heads", "valves", "controllers", "parts", "replacement"
  • Job seekers: "irrigation jobs", "careers", "hiring", "salary"

These negatives typically save $300-600 per month for a typical irrigation Google Ads account running $2,500-4,000/month.

Not all irrigation services have the same margins, urgency, or customer intent. FieldSprout automatically structures campaigns by service type:

Campaign 4: Spring Activation (Peak: March-April)

  • Keywords: "spring startup sprinklers", "turn on irrigation system", "irrigation spring activation", "sprinkler system check"
  • Match types: Phrase match

  • Landing page: Spring activation service page
  • Budget: Peaks March-April (post-winter startup)

4. Geographic Targeting for Service Areas

Not all service calls are profitable. A 40-minute drive for a $150 sprinkler head repair loses money when you factor in drive time and fuel.

The Campaign Manager uses ZIP-level targeting to:

  • Increase bids for ZIPs within 20 minutes of your location
  • Decrease bids for edge-of-service-area ZIPs
  • Block entirely if you're consistently unprofitable in certain areas
  • Target higher-value neighborhoods for installation campaigns

The Ad Copy Agent continuously tests irrigation-specific messaging patterns:

  • Service guarantees: "1-Year Warranty on All Installations" vs. "100% Satisfaction Guaranteed"
  • Pricing signals: "Free Estimates" vs. "Installation from $2,500"

Common Irrigation Google Ads Questions

Should I run Google Ads in winter?

In cold climates: Reduce budgets by 60-80% but don't pause entirely. Focus on planning/consultation for spring installations and equipment upgrade opportunities.

In warm climates (Arizona, Texas, Florida, Southern California): Run year-round with minor seasonal adjustments. These markets have 12-month irrigation usage.

How much should irrigation companies spend on Google Ads?

A typical rule of thumb: $30-60 per lead for repairs, $75-150 per lead for installations. If your average installation generates $4,000 in revenue at 40% margin, you can afford $100-200 per lead and still be profitable.

Most irrigation companies spend $2,000-5,000 per month on Google Ads during peak season (March-September), scaling down to $500-1,500 during off-season.

What's a good conversion rate for irrigation Google Ads?

Repair campaigns: 10-18% conversion rate (form fill or call)
Installation campaigns: 5-10% conversion rate
Winterization/activation campaigns: 12-20% conversion rate (seasonal urgency drives action)

If you're below these benchmarks, likely issues:

  • Irrelevant traffic (poor keyword targeting or missing negative keywords)
  • Weak landing pages (slow load time, poor mobile experience, unclear CTA)
  • Misaligned ad copy (promise doesn't match landing page)

Should irrigation companies use Google Local Services Ads?

Yes, especially for repair services. Google Local Services Ads (the "Google Guaranteed" listings) are extremely effective for irrigation contractors. They appear above regular Google Ads and you only pay per lead, not per click.

FieldSprout manages both Google Ads and Google Local Services Ads in a coordinated strategy. LSAs handle branded and high-intent repair searches, while Google Ads expand reach for installations and seasonal services.
