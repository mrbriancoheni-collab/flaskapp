Why Lawn Care Google Ads Are Different

Lawn care companies face unique Google Ads challenges that generic marketing agencies don't understand. Your business is highly seasonal—demand peaks from April through October, then drops dramatically in winter. Your most profitable customers aren't one-time mows; they're seasonal contracts that generate $1,200-3,000 in recurring revenue over 6-8 months.

Generic Google Ads strategies fail because they don't distinguish between a homeowner searching "lawn mowing near me" who wants weekly service all summer versus someone who just needs their grass cut once before a backyard party. You need campaigns optimized for contract signups, not just one-time jobs. And you need aggressive negative keywords to block the massive volume of DIY searchers and people shopping for lawn mowers.

The Lawn Care Google Ads Problem: Low-Value Clicks

Most lawn care companies waste 30-50% of their Google Ads budget on clicks that will never convert to profitable customers. They attract homeowners researching how to do it themselves, people shopping for lawn equipment, job seekers looking for landscape work, and searchers outside their service area.

The most common lawn care Google Ads mistakes:

  • Running flat budgets year-round instead of scaling up for spring signup season
  • Mixing one-time and recurring services in the same campaigns
  • Broad match keywords like "lawn care" that attract equipment shoppers
  • No negative keywords for "mower", "DIY", "equipment", "jobs"
  • Generic ad copy that doesn't emphasize recurring contracts
  • No route-based targeting to keep jobs geographically clustered

3. Lawn Care-Specific Negative Keyword Strategy

The Negative Keyword Agent knows lawn care search patterns. It automatically blocks:

  • Equipment shoppers: "mower", "lawn mower", "riding mower", "zero turn", "trimmer", "blower"
  • DIY searches: "DIY", "how to", "tips", "advice", "myself", "YouTube"
  • Parts and supplies: "parts", "blades", "oil", "gas", "fertilizer" (when selling products, not service)
  • Job seekers: "jobs", "careers", "hiring", "salary", "work for", "employment"
  • Commercial/large property: "commercial", "acreage", "farm", "ranch" (unless you serve commercial)

These negative keywords typically save $300-600 per month in wasted spend for a typical lawn care Google Ads account running $2,000-4,000/month.

4. Route-Based Geographic Targeting

Lawn care profitability depends on routing efficiency. A $40 mowing job 30 minutes away loses money, but the same job on an existing route is highly profitable. FieldSprout's Campaign Manager uses ZIP-level targeting to:

  • Increase bids for ZIPs where you already have 5+ customers (route density)
  • Moderate bids for adjacent ZIPs that connect existing routes
  • Decrease or block bids for isolated areas that hurt routing efficiency

This "route-first" bidding strategy can improve crew efficiency by 15-25% while maintaining the same ad spend.

Common Lawn Care Google Ads Questions

Should I advertise lawn care services in winter?

In most climates, yes, but with minimal budget. Focus on snow removal (if applicable), early spring signup discounts, or one-time cleanups. In warm climates (Florida, Texas, Southern California), lawn care is year-round.

FieldSprout automatically adjusts budgets to near-zero in true off-season months while maintaining minimal presence for planning searches like "lawn care service for spring."

How much should lawn care companies spend on Google Ads?

A typical rule of thumb: $15-30 per lead target for one-time jobs, $40-80 per lead for seasonal contracts. If your average seasonal contract generates $1,500 in revenue at 60% margin, you can afford $60-100 per contract acquisition and maintain healthy margins.

Most lawn care companies spend $1,500-4,000 per month on Google Ads during peak season (March-June), scaling down to $300-800 during off-season.

What's a good conversion rate for lawn care Google Ads?

Seasonal contract campaigns: 6-12% conversion rate (form fill or call)
One-time mowing campaigns: 8-15% conversion rate
Fertilization programs: 5-10% conversion rate

If you're below these benchmarks, you likely have issues with:

  • Irrelevant traffic (poor keyword targeting or missing negative keywords for equipment)
  • Weak landing pages (no clear pricing, slow mobile load time, unclear service area)
  • Misaligned ad copy (promise doesn't match landing page offer)

Should lawn care companies use Google Local Services Ads (LSAs)?

Yes. Google Local Services Ads (the "Google Guaranteed" listings) are very effective for lawn care companies. They appear above regular Google Ads and you only pay per lead, not per click. LSAs work especially well for "near me" searches.

FieldSprout manages both Google Ads and Google Local Services Ads in a coordinated strategy. LSAs handle branded and "near me" searches, while Google Ads expand reach for specific services like fertilization and aeration.
