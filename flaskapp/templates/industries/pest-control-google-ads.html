Why Pest Control Google Ads Are Different

Pest control companies face unique Google Ads challenges. Your business is highly seasonal—mosquito and ant searches peak in spring/summer, termite swarms happen March-May, and rodent problems spike in fall/winter. Your customers have different urgency levels—bed bugs and termites require immediate treatment, while general pest control is often preventive.

Generic Google Ads strategies fail for pest control because they don't account for seasonal pest patterns, one-time vs. recurring service models, and the massive difference between a $150 one-time ant treatment and a $400/year recurring service contract worth $2,000+ lifetime value.

The Pest Control Google Ads Problem: DIY Searchers

Most pest control companies waste 40-60% of their Google Ads budget on people who have zero intention of hiring a professional. They're searching to identify bugs, looking for natural home remedies, or planning DIY treatments. These searchers click your ad, waste your money, and never convert.

The most common pest control Google Ads mistakes:

  • No negative keywords for "DIY", "natural", "home remedy", "identify", "pictures"
  • Broad match keywords like "ants" or "bugs" that attract identification searches
  • Mixing one-time and recurring services in the same campaigns
  • Running flat budgets year-round instead of seasonal adjustments
  • Generic ad copy that doesn't differentiate prevention vs. elimination
  • No geographic targeting to focus on profitable service areas

3. Pest Control-Specific Negative Keyword Strategy

The Negative Keyword Agent knows pest control search patterns and automatically blocks:

  • DIY searches: "DIY", "natural", "home remedy", "essential oils", "vinegar", "baking soda"
  • Identification queries: "what kind", "identify", "pictures", "images", "look like"

Common Pest Control Google Ads Questions

Should pest control companies bid on competitor names?

Generally no, unless you're significantly cheaper or offer better guarantees. Competitor keyword bidding works better for commoditized services. For pest control, focus budget on pest-specific keywords with higher intent.

How much should pest control companies spend on Google Ads?

Target cost per acquisition: $40-80 per one-time treatment, $80-150 per recurring service signup.

Most pest control companies spend $2,000-5,000 per month on Google Ads, scaling up during peak pest seasons (spring/summer) and down during slower seasons (winter for mosquitoes, summer for rodents).

What's a good conversion rate for pest control Google Ads?

Recurring service campaigns: 6-12% conversion rate
Termite services: 8-14% conversion rate (high urgency)
Bed bug treatment: 10-16% conversion rate (extreme urgency)
One-time treatments: 5-10% conversion rate

Should pest control companies use remarketing?

Absolutely. Many pest control searchers research multiple companies before deciding. Remarketing keeps your brand visible and offers second-chance conversions at lower cost. Particularly effective for high-value services like termite treatments and recurring contracts.
