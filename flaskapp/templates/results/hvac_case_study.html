The situation

An 8-truck HVAC company operating in a mid-sized metro market had been running Google Ads for two years. Monthly ad spend: $3,200. Agency management fee: $1,800/month. Total monthly marketing outlay: $5,000.

The owner couldn't answer a basic question: which campaigns were actually booking jobs? The agency sent monthly reports with impression share, click-through rate, and average CPC. None of it connected to the field schedule. When summer came, the owner increased ad spend by $800/month based on instinct, not data.

When RevForge connected to the account, the first automated audit took 4 minutes. What it found explained everything.

What RevForge found on day 1

Specific. Concrete. Fixable.

847 wasted impressions on "HVAC school"
The account had zero negative keywords. Terms like "HVAC school near me," "HVAC certification course," "become an HVAC tech," and "free HVAC training" were all triggering ads. These clicks have zero probability of booking a service call — and the account was paying for them.
Ads running 2am–6am — zero conversions in 90 days
No call schedule had been applied to the campaigns. Ads ran 24/7. In the prior 90 days, the 2am–6am window had generated 0 conversions out of 47 clicks. That's roughly $280/month in confirmed waste on clicks that couldn't possibly book a job — the phones were off.
Quality Score averaging 4.2 across 3 campaigns
A Quality Score of 4.2 means Google views this account as poorly relevant. At that QS, the company was paying roughly 50–60% more per click than a competitor with a QS of 7+. The campaigns had generic ad copy mismatched with specific keyword intent — "AC Repair" keywords sending to a generic homepage.
No negative keyword list — at all
After two years and $76,800 in total ad spend, the account had zero shared negative keyword lists and 3 campaign-level negatives total. This is the single most common finding across new RevForge accounts. Most agencies set up campaigns and don't maintain negatives. It's not dramatic — it's just neglect that compounds monthly.

Why this works — the mechanism, not the marketing

Three reasons most HVAC accounts improve within 30 days.

Most HVAC accounts have never been cleaned

The biggest gains come from removing waste, not adding spend. The average new RevForge HVAC account has 3–7 negative keywords on day one. Adding 200–400 targeted negatives immediately redirects 25–40% of budget to searches that actually convert. The improvement shows up in cost per lead within weeks — not because of clever bidding but because of basic hygiene that wasn't being done.

Daily optimization compounds faster than monthly optimization

When an agency makes a change on the 1st and again on the 30th, you get 2 data cycles per month. RevForge makes adjustments every 24 hours — 30 data cycles per month. Each cycle tightens bids, catches new wasted terms, and adjusts to shifts in competitor behavior. The compounding effect of daily cycles versus monthly cycles is the main driver of the 39% CPL reduction in 31 days.

Quality Score improvement cuts CPC without cutting bids

Google rewards relevance with lower cost per click. A Quality Score of 7 vs 4 means paying 30–50% less for the same position. Most accounts are stuck at QS 3–5 because keyword-to-ad-to-landing page alignment was never set up correctly. Fixing this is not fast — it takes 2–4 weeks for Google to update scores — but the CPC reduction it produces lasts indefinitely and multiplies the effect of every other optimization.
